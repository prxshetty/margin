# Chapter 3

The studio door creaked open as Elara stepped inside, the morning light filtering through dusty windows and illuminating the chaotic masterpiece drying in the center of the room. The raw, visceral creation dominated the space—violent brushstrokes, lurid colors, and unapologetic energy that seemed to pulse with life even still wet. The air hung heavy with the sharp scent of turpentine and paint, mixing with the faint metallic tang of blood where Elara had nicked her finger with a palette knife in her frenzy.

"Wow," she breathed, running a hand through her paint-streaked hair. "It's still here. I didn't dream it."

Kaelen leaned against the doorframe, arms crossed as his eyes swept from Elara to the canvas and back again. "Of course it's still here. The truth doesn't evaporate with sunlight."

Lena stepped inside behind them, her analytical gaze immediately cataloging the scene—the overturned jars, the smeared footprints, the canvas that seemed to shift and writhe in the changing light. "Your blood pressure is still dangerously elevated," she noted, "and your pupils remain dilated beyond normal parameters for this light condition."

The sound of expensive leather shoes on concrete announced Julian's arrival before he appeared in the doorway. "Elara! Thank God I caught you." He stepped inside, followed immediately by a man in his late fifties with silver hair and a bespoke suit that seemed to absorb all the light in the room. This was the collector—Vincent Moreau, whose name was whispered in reverence across auction houses from Basel to Hong Kong.

Julian froze mid-stride as his eyes landed on the canvas in the center of the room. His face went through a rapid succession of emotions—confusion, horror, then something that looked dangerously like panic. "What... what is this? Where is 'Ethereal Cascade'?" He rushed to the canvas, touching it tentatively with a manicured finger before recoiling as if burned. "This is... raw. Unfinished. It's not what we discussed."

Vincent Moreau approached more slowly, his eyes narrowing as he circled the canvas like a predator studying prey. Unlike Julian, he seemed intrigued rather than horrified. "Interesting," he murmured, his accent barely perceptible. "There's a violence here I haven't seen in your work before, Ms. Vance."

"Wow, you noticed," Elara said, her voice dripping with sarcasm as she stepped between the collector and her painting. "It's almost as if I was trying to express something."

Kaelen snorted from the doorway. "She's been serving you people watered-down cocktails for two years while keeping the good stuff locked in the back. Now that she's finally poured you a real drink, you're complaining about the proof."
